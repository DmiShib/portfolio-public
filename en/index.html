The Education Paradox

I have been in GameDev for 15 years, with 10+ years specializing in Unity (C#). Upon entering college, I quickly realized that the curriculum wasn't bringing me closer to my dream, so I took ownership of my education.

I studied C# and Unity right during lectures, and at home, I turned theory into practice. This persistence led to a unique situation. While still a student, I secured a job at a company affiliated with the region's leading technical university. I developed complex 3D simulators for higher education students who were academically senior to me. This experience taught me the most important lesson: real skills and results matter more than formal credentials.

The Self-Made Approach

I believe the best teacher is curiosity multiplied by an engineering system.

  • Engineering Efficiency: If I spot a routine task, I don't just execute it—I build automation tools. For instance, I wrote my own software to structure learning materials to absorb new technologies faster.
  • Fundamental Base: My mentors were not only hundreds of completed courses but also classic books like "Clean Code" and "CLR via C#". They shaped my architectural thinking.
  • T-Shaped Growth: I never limited myself to just being a coder. If a project needs a shader, I learn HLSL. If it needs marketing, I dive into ASO. I strive to understand the product as a whole.

From Coder to Technical Leader

This approach ensured rapid growth in my career. At Playgendary, I grew from a Unity Developer to a role effectively acting as a Technical Owner on major commercial projects. I learned to perform reverse engineering, recover lost knowledge, and build development processes from scratch.

Today, this experience works at Grindset Studios. This is my R&D testing ground where I create technological ecosystems and release full-cycle products. This project serves as living proof that I can turn chaos into a system.

Whether it is low-level optimization or gameplay polish, I approach every task with a founder's mindset and the same drive that once compelled me to start this path on my own.
